2. What Ferralis does

Ferralis builds and adapts a strength-training programme from what you tell it, guides your sessions, records your loads and your history, and offers general nutrition guidelines. The Apple Watch app accompanies your sessions. From version 1.6, Ferralis can also, if you turn it on, read the cardio sessions recorded in the Health app to display them and organise your programme around them, and guide interval sessions.

Ferralis works without an account. Some features require a subscription or the lifetime purchase: they are described in the purchase terms. Without a purchase, the app can still be used as a day log.

3. Warning: for sport, not medical use

  • Ferralis is not a medical device. It makes no diagnosis, monitors no health condition and offers no treatment. Its programmes, nutrition guidelines and texts are general training information, not personalised medical or dietary advice.
  • Talk to a doctor before starting or resuming strength training, particularly if you have a health problem, are taking medication, are pregnant or are returning after a break, and before any decision that affects your health.
  • Listen to your body. Stop the session if you feel unwell or notice unusual discomfort, abnormal breathlessness or dizziness, and see a doctor. The suggested loads and reps are suggestions: you decide what you lift, with a technique you have mastered and, if needed, the help of a training partner or coach.
  • Areas to go easy on simply leave out exercises that work the area you have chosen; they measure nothing and treat nothing.
  • Nutrition guidelines are not intended for people who have an eating disorder, are pregnant or are breastfeeding. They are not displayed under the age of 18.
  • Data from the Health app (weight, height, steps and, from version 1.6, heart rate, active energy, distance, pace, route) is measured by your devices and provided by Apple. Ferralis displays it as it is, without interpretation, and does not guarantee its accuracy.
  • Train in a safe place. When you are out and about, do not look at your screen while moving and follow the highway code.
