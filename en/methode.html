Method

Establish before you build

Four stages. They follow in this order because each one makes the next decidable — that is the only reason they are numbered.

  1. Establish

    Read the funnel data as it is, go through the journey myself on a real phone, listen to the teams who receive the complaints. We look for where people stop, not for where the interface displeases.

    Output: the drop-off points, quantified and ranked.

  2. Decide

    Not everything gets fixed. We keep what has a measurable effect and a known cost, and set the rest aside, writing down why — that is what stops it being prescribed again six months later.

    Output: a short, ordered list, with the expected effect and what was set aside.

  3. Specify

    Specifications a development team can carry out without coming back with three questions: business rules, edge cases, error states, and acceptance criteria that can be checked one by one.

    Output: functional specifications and acceptance criteria.

  4. Deliver and measure

    I stay until production. What is not measured afterwards has not been delivered: we check whether the drop-off point has moved, and we accept the verdict, including when it is negative.

    Output: the production release, and the gap between the expected effect and the observed effect.

How I work day to day

  • In your tools and your rituals, not in a parallel setup.
  • In writing: a decision that is not written down has not been made.
  • In disagreement when needed. You are not paying me to approve a plan.
  • With nothing held back: what I produce stays with you, usable without me.
